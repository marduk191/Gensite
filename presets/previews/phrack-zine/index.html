phile 01 — Introduction: why we still write in plain text 
                       ----[ Introduction ]----

A text file opens everywhere. It survives every format war, every
redesign and every forgotten login. That is the whole pitch.

This issue is made of small, readable things: a cable, a menu, a
story, a history. Nothing here needs a special viewer. If you can
read this line you can read the rest.

Send us your own philes. Keep them under 80 columns.

                                            -- ghost_in_cron
                                  [demo author, fictional]
phile 02 — Soldering your first serial cable 
Parts list (all hobby-shop stuff, all harmless):
  1 x 9-pin connector      1 x 25-pin connector
  1 x length of 3-core wire   flux, solder, patience

Step 1. Tin the pins before you try to join anything.
Step 2. Heat the pad, not the wire. Count to three.
Step 3. Label the ends. You will forget which is which.

If it smells like burning plastic you held it too long.
Open a window. Try again tomorrow.   -- tin_wren (demo)
phile 03 — A field guide to dial tones (history) 
Before the screech of a handshake there was a hum: the dial tone,
two steady notes laid over each other so a switch could say
"go ahead". Later came the chirps, the carrier, the pause, the
long wait while two machines argued about speed.

We collect the sounds because they were the doorbell of an era.
Record yours, label the date, and send it in.
                                             -- modemoth (demo)
phile 04 — Writing a text-mode menu in 60 lines 
A menu is a loop: draw, read one key, act, draw again.

  while true
      clear screen, print the choices
      key = read one character
      if key is "q" then stop
      otherwise run the matching action

The rest is taste. Box characters, colours, a hint line at the
bottom. Keep every action reachable by a single key.
                                             -- pica_pica (demo)
phile 05 — Loopback: a short story 
At 3 a.m. the terminal said hello back before she typed it.
She checked the cable. She checked the clock. She typed "who is
this" and the answer came in her own handwriting, slowed down a
little, as if someone were learning her.

By morning the phone was ringing in a house with no phone.

                                    -- static_sparrow (fiction, demo)
