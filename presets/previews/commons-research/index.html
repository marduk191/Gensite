Research profile · Demonstration only

Making environmental information useful in everyday life.

I study how people notice, discuss and act on the places around them. This fictional profile shows how to present a research agenda without burying the work.

All projects below are invented examples. They are not published papers, measured findings or claims of institutional support.

Mira Vale · Fictional working concept

Exploring how a local map might describe the shade people actually experience while walking.

Mira Vale and the fictional Waterside group

A plain-language interface concept for explaining changing river conditions.

The concept explores a small set of questions: what changed, what is uncertain, and where can a reader verify the source? A real project would link official observations and separate safety advice from interpretation.

What a map leaves out

Mira Vale · Fictional methods note

Questions for designing a participatory mapping session with room for disagreement.

Read the abstract

A map can make one kind of knowledge visible while hiding another. This sample note outlines prompts for discussing missing routes, uncertain boundaries and the limits of a shared legend. It reports no participant data.
