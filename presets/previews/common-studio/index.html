What we help untangle

Finding the story

We turn a pile of notes into a useful narrative: what you do, who it helps, and why it matters. In this fictional service, the output is a story map and a short messaging guide.

Giving the idea a name

Explore language territories, develop a focused shortlist, and test how each option sounds in real sentences. Naming concepts are creative starting points, not trademark or legal advice.

Putting words to work

Build a small, consistent set of pages: a clear introduction, a useful explanation, and an honest next step. No invented proof points or borrowed customer praise.

A useful brief starts with a real question.

What is hard to explain? Who needs to understand it? What should they be able to do next?

This demo does not collect inquiries. Use the notes below as a starting point for your own project.

Read the sample brief

Listen closely

Gather the actual language people use. Ask where the explanation gets stuck before deciding what it ought to sound like.

Make it tangible

Write a first version early. A real paragraph is easier to discuss than a page of adjectives.

Leave it usable

Document the choices so the next person can write with confidence, without needing the original author in the room.

Sample brief: a neighborhood repair library

Fictional project: explain how residents can borrow tools and learn simple repairs. The page should make borrowing rules readable, separate safety guidance from promotional copy, and show an honest contact route once real details exist.
